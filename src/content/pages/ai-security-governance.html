Why now

AI Is Moving Faster Than Its Controls

AI adoption is outpacing security and governance. In IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach report, 13% of organizations reported breaches of AI models or applications, and 97% of those lacked proper AI access controls. One in five reported a breach due to shadow AI, and high levels of shadow AI added an average of $670,000 to breach costs. (IBM, July 2025)

Regulation is arriving too. Under the EU AI Act, transparency obligations have applied since 2 August 2026, and after the 2026 Digital Omnibus amendment, obligations for high-risk AI systems apply from 2 December 2027 (2 August 2028 for AI built into regulated products). (DLA Piper; Sidley)

Questions buyers ask

AI Security & Governance: what buyers ask us

What is AI governance?

AI governance is the set of policies, roles, processes and controls that make sure an organization's AI is used safely, legally and as intended. It covers knowing what AI you have, assessing its risk, approving it, monitoring it in production and keeping evidence that it meets internal and regulatory requirements.

What is the difference between AI governance and data governance?

Data governance manages the data itself: ownership, quality, lineage, privacy and access. AI governance manages how AI systems use that data and behave: risk, approvals, transparency, monitoring and accountability. Because AI depends on data, the two work best as one program, which is why we deliver them together.

What do AI security services include?

AI security services protect AI systems and the data they use. They include threat modeling, guardrails against prompt injection and harmful outputs, least-privilege access for models and agents, data leakage prevention, shadow AI discovery, security monitoring and incident response for AI, plus red-team testing.

What are data governance services?

Data governance services set up and run the people, processes and tools that keep enterprise data trustworthy: data catalogs, business glossaries, lineage, quality management, privacy classification and access governance. For AI, they make sure models and assistants only use accurate data that people are allowed to see.

Does the EU AI Act apply to our company?

It can apply to organizations outside the EU if they place AI systems on the EU market or their AI's outputs are used in the EU. Obligations depend on your role (provider or deployer) and each system's risk level. Our assessment maps your AI systems against the Act. This is general information, not legal advice.

When do the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations apply?

After the 2026 Digital Omnibus amendment, obligations for high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III apply from 2 December 2027, and for AI built into regulated products from 2 August 2028. Transparency obligations have applied since 2 August 2026. Check current guidance, as timelines can change.

What is ISO/IEC 42001, and do we need it?

ISO/IEC 42001 is the international standard for an AI management system: the policies, risk processes and controls for developing or using AI responsibly. It is voluntary, but certification is increasingly requested by enterprise customers and helps demonstrate EU AI Act readiness.

How do you protect LLM applications from prompt injection?

We combine several defences: input filtering, separating trusted instructions from untrusted content, least-privilege tool access, output checks before any action, human approval for high-risk steps and red-team testing. No single control is enough, so we layer them and monitor for new attack patterns.

How do you find shadow AI in our organization?

We analyze network, identity and expense data, browser and SaaS usage, and survey teams to find the AI tools in use. Then we assess each tool's data exposure and risk, and offer approved, governed alternatives so people keep the productivity without the risk.

Will governance slow down our AI projects?

Done well, it speeds them up. A clear risk classification and approval path means teams know what is needed from the start, and security and compliance reviews stop being last-minute blockers. Built-in controls also make releases repeatable.

What evidence do you provide for auditors and regulators?

We maintain an evidence pack for each AI system: inventory entry, risk assessment, data lineage, model documentation, test and evaluation results, approvals, monitoring records and incident logs, mapped to the frameworks you answer to, such as the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 or SOC 2.
