How we evaluate AI

How We Evaluate AI: A Repeatable Method

Every engagement follows the same evaluation loop, so results are comparable release after release.

1Define success
2Build test sets
3Run evaluations
4Gate the release
5Monitor in production
6Learn from failures
Release gatePass or block
  1. Define success. Agree with your business, risk and compliance owners what "good" means for this use case, and the thresholds for release.
  2. Build test sets. A golden dataset of real cases with expected answers, plus edge cases and adversarial prompts.
  3. Run evaluations. Automated metrics, LLM-as-judge scoring calibrated against human reviewers, and expert human review where it matters.
  4. Gate the release. Results are compared to thresholds; anything that misses a gate is blocked, with the failing cases shown.
  5. Monitor in production. Live traffic is sampled and scored continuously.
  6. Learn from failures. Every new failure becomes a test case, so the test set gets stronger over time.

AI red teaming

AI Red Teaming: We Attack Your AI Before Someone Else Does

Our red team tries to make your AI misbehave the way a real attacker or careless user would, using the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications as a baseline and adding attacks specific to your use case.

  • Prompt injection and jailbreaks. direct and indirect (hidden in documents, emails or web pages).
  • Sensitive data leakage. attempts to extract personal data, secrets or other users' information.
  • Unsafe tool use by agents. tricking an agent into actions outside its permissions.
  • Harmful or off-policy content. outputs that breach your policies, brand or regulations.
  • Model and system abuse. excessive usage, denial-of-wallet and manipulation of retrieval sources.

Deliverable: a findings report rated by severity, with reproducible examples and recommended fixes, plus a re-test after fixes.

Questions buyers ask

AI Testing Services: what buyers ask us

What are AI testing services?

AI testing services evaluate AI systems (machine learning models, LLM and RAG applications, and AI agents) for accuracy, safety, fairness, robustness, performance and compliance. Unlike traditional QA, they measure behavior across many realistic and adversarial cases against agreed thresholds, before release and continuously in production.

How do you test LLM applications?

We agree success criteria, build a golden dataset of real questions with expected answers, add edge cases and adversarial prompts, then score outputs using automated metrics, LLM-as-judge calibrated against human reviewers, and expert review. Results are compared with release thresholds, and the tests run again on every change.

How do you measure hallucination?

We check each answer's claims against the source documents or data it should rely on. The share of answers containing unsupported claims is the hallucination rate, and the share fully supported is groundedness. Both are tracked per release and on sampled live traffic.

What is AI red teaming?

AI red teaming is structured attack testing of an AI system. Testers try prompt injection, jailbreaks, data extraction, unsafe agent actions and harmful outputs, the way real attackers or careless users would, then report findings by severity with reproducible examples and fixes.

How do you test AI agents?

We replay real historical cases and scenario scripts, then measure task success, whether the right tools were called with the right parameters, how exceptions were handled, and whether the agent escalated when it should. We also red-team agents for unsafe or out-of-scope actions.

How do you test AI for bias?

We compare performance and outcomes across relevant groups, such as age, gender or region where lawful and appropriate, using fairness metrics agreed with your risk team. Gaps above the threshold are investigated back to data or model causes, fixed and re-tested.

What is the difference between AI testing and AI-powered test automation?

AI testing checks whether an AI system behaves correctly. AI-powered test automation uses AI to test ordinary software faster, for example by generating test cases or self-healing scripts. We offer both; the second is covered on our AI Quality Engineering page.

How long does AI testing take?

An initial evaluation and red-team assessment of one AI system typically takes [2–4] weeks; broader programs take [2–8] weeks depending on the number of systems, data access and compliance needs. After that, continuous evaluation runs automatically with every release.

Can you integrate AI testing into our existing QA and CI/CD?

Yes. We add AI evaluation suites to your existing pipelines and test management tools, so model, prompt and data changes are tested automatically alongside your regular test runs, with human review where needed.

Can you provide documentation for compliance audits?

Yes. We provide test strategies, release-gate definitions, results, red-team findings and re-test evidence, mapped to frameworks such as the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF and sector regulations, ready for auditors and your governance program.

How do you handle personal data during testing?

We avoid live personal data wherever possible, using anonymized, masked or synthetic data. Where real data is needed, it stays in your environment under restricted access, with audit trails of every test run.
