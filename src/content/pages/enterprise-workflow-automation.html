The hidden cost of manual work

Manual Work Is Slowing Your Business Down

Every day, skilled people copy data from emails into systems, chase missing documents, check the same rules by hand and pass work between teams by spreadsheet. Each handoff adds delay and risk of error. Customers wait, backlogs grow and costs rise with every extra case.

Earlier automation often made it only part of the way. Scripts and bots handle one task but break when a screen changes, and they can't read a messy document or make a judgment call, so people still sit in the middle.

The goal isn't more bots. It's processes that run themselves, with people in control.

How we automate a workflow end to end

How an Automated Workflow Runs, End to End

Take an everyday example: supplier invoices arriving by email.

01Capture

The invoice arrives by email, portal or EDI and is picked up automatically.

02Understand

Document AI reads it and extracts supplier, amounts, PO number and line items.

03Decide

Rules and models match it to the PO and receipt and check for duplicates and fraud signals.

04Act

Matched invoices are posted to the ERP and scheduled for payment, through APIs or RPA where no API exists.

05Handle exceptions

Mismatches go to an AI agent that investigates and resolves what it can; the rest go to the right person with all context attached.

06Measure

Every case is logged, so cycle time, touchless rate and exceptions are visible on a live dashboard.

Questions buyers ask

Enterprise Workflow Automation: what buyers ask us

What is enterprise workflow automation?

Enterprise workflow automation uses software to run business processes from start to finish across systems and teams: capturing inputs, applying rules and decisions, updating systems and routing exceptions to people. Modern approaches combine integration, document AI, rules, RPA and AI agents, each used where it fits best.

What is the difference between RPA and AI agents?

RPA follows fixed, step-by-step scripts and works best for stable, rule-based tasks, often on legacy screens. AI agents can read unstructured information, reason about exceptions and choose their next step. Most processes benefit from both: RPA or APIs for the predictable steps, agents for the judgment calls.

What is hyperautomation?

Hyperautomation is the coordinated use of several automation technologies (workflow orchestration, integration, RPA, document AI, machine learning and AI agents) to automate as much of a business process as is sensible, with governance and measurement across all of them.

Which processes are best to automate first?

High-volume, repetitive processes with clear outcomes and measurable cost or delay, such as invoice processing, onboarding or service requests. We score candidates on volume, variability, systems, risk and value, and usually start with one that pays back quickly and builds reusable components.

Will automation work with our existing systems?

Yes. We connect through APIs and integration platforms where available, use RPA for legacy systems without APIs, and build on the workflow, RPA or low-code platforms you already own where they fit.

Our RPA bots keep breaking. Can you help?

Yes. We assess existing bots, fix the most valuable ones, and replace fragile screen-based steps with API integrations, document AI or agents where that's more robust, reducing maintenance and raising the share of cases handled end to end.

How do you calculate automation ROI?

We measure the baseline (volume, handling time, error and rework rates, cycle time) before building, then track the same metrics after go-live, along with run costs. ROI combines hours released, faster cycle times, fewer errors and better customer outcomes.

How long does it take to automate a process?

The Automation Discovery Sprint takes [3–4] weeks, including a working pilot. A full end-to-end process typically goes live in [8–16] weeks depending on systems and exceptions. Later processes are faster because integrations and components are reused.

How do you keep automated processes secure and compliant?

Automations run with least-privilege service accounts, store credentials securely, log every action and keep humans in the loop for approvals that need them. Governance gives each automation an owner, documentation and an audit trail.

What support do you provide after go-live?

We monitor automations, handle failures and changes in connected systems, report on touchless rate, exceptions and cycle time, and keep improving and extending the workflows, under an agreed managed service.
